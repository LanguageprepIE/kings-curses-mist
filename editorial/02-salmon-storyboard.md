# Chapter 02 — The Salmon of Knowledge / El salmón del conocimiento
**Status:** editorial plan for David's review; **not yet approved or published**.  
**Collection:** [LIBRO] Of Kings, Curses and Mist. No changes to [PERGAMINOS].

## Editorial approach
A coming-of-age legend about curiosity, patient teaching, thwarted expectations and the difference between *possessing knowledge* and *using wisdom*. The story should be enchanting, not a moralising fable. Fionn is observant and eager, not a precocious know-it-all; Finegas is a respected poet with a human disappointment, not a villain. Their final exchange should be tender and nuanced.

- Separate, literary English and peninsular Spanish versions, each approximately **1,150–1,300 words**; natural adaptation, not literal translation.
- Suggested reading age: secondary students. Six narrative scenes; calm but vivid prose, varied rhythm, sensory Boyne landscape.
- Preserve core tradition: young Fionn (also named Demne in early texts); poet Finnéces/Finegas; River Boyne; seven-year watch; prophetic name; salmon caught; Fionn cooks it, burns his thumb and puts it in his mouth; knowledge passes to him; Finegas recognises the event and releases him to his path.
- Tradition varies: do not present the nine hazels, mystical well and the exact origin of the fish as uniformly attested details of this particular episode. If included, present as poetic lore and unpack in *Beyond the Legend*.
- Naming policy in the narrative: **Fionn** and **Finegas**; explain Finn/Finnéces/Demne and Irish **An Bradán Feasa** in the cultural note. Avoid turning the tale into a full Fianna biography.

## After the Story / Después de la historia
Three multiple-choice comprehension questions (with immediate, supportive feedback), independently written in both languages:
1. Why did Finegas wait beside the Boyne for seven years? / ¿Por qué esperó Finegas durante siete años junto al Boyne?
2. How did Fionn first taste the salmon? / ¿Cómo probó Fionn el salmón por primera vez?
3. What did Finegas decide once he understood what had happened? / ¿Qué decidió Finegas al comprender lo ocurrido?

**Optional reflection:** *Finegas spent years waiting for a gift that went to someone else. What makes his response difficult—and admirable?* / *Finegas llevaba años esperando un don que recibió otra persona. ¿Por qué resulta tan difícil —y tan valiosa— su reacción?*
Avoid saying knowledge automatically makes someone morally good.

## Beyond the Legend / Más allá de la leyenda
Four titled short notes, about 70–110 words each, flexible across one or two book pages if needed:

1. **A hero before he was a hero / Antes de convertirse en héroe.** Fionn mac Cumhaill and the Fenian Cycle; this story belongs to accounts of his youth, not an isolated historical event.
2. **The Boyne: a river of stories / El Boyne, un río de leyendas.** Real River Boyne and the imagined pool; hazels, poetic inspiration and the links of water/knowledge in Irish myth. Distinguish storytelling tradition from geography.
3. **The thumb of knowledge / El pulgar del conocimiento.** The accidental burn and later thumb-to-mouth insight; oral and textual variation. Clarify that knowledge and wisdom are not synonyms.
4. **The poet and the prophecy / El poeta y la profecía.** Finnéces/Finegas, Fionn/Finn/Demne, prophecy and the name's ambiguity; the teacher's response is a key interpretive choice, with some modern dialogue invented.

## Points for David's review
- Suggested protagonist arc: knowledge is received by chance, but wisdom must still be cultivated.
- Finegas should retain his sadness for the lost dream; his generosity matters precisely because the loss is real.
- The ending should open the larger Fionn cycle without giving away later adventures.
