# Art direction and continuity bible — v0.1

Status: **working document, to be approved by David and Ann**. Applies across all 20 stories.

## Collection identity

**Of Kings, Curses and Mist** — *Where Ancient Ireland Comes Alive*  
Spanish: **De reyes, maldiciones y niebla** — *Donde la Irlanda ancestral cobra vida*

- Audience: teenage and general readers; mature, not childish.
- Art: traditional-looking digital oil/gouache painting, tactile brushwork, naturalistic figures, evocative Irish landscape.
- Palette: forest green #193B35; muted green #375E56; aged gold #C4A66A; parchment #E9DEC5; Atlantic blue #354B65.
- Celtic manuscript ornament appears in editorial borders and titles, not as distracting overlays.
- Lighting may change across stories; palette and brush texture remain coherent. No obligatory atmospheric darkness.
- Text must sit on its own solid, legible paper-like surface; do not lay body paragraphs directly on busy pictures.
- Main reader: double-page spread (illustration left, text right) on wider screens; single-column reading on mobile.
- Transitions: subtle page-turn feeling; honour reduced-motion preferences and allow no animation.

## Narrative accuracy and continuity gates

1. Read and outline the source tradition before planning each scene.
2. Approve a scene-by-scene character roster and transformation state before image generation.
3. Make one approved character reference portrait per recurring figure and preserve it in later scenes.
4. Check number of characters, identity, age, costume, physical relationships, location and sequence **manually** after generation.
5. Character changes must have a story reason (ageing, magic, disguise), with their appearance explicitly tracked.
6. Never infer historical costume details from AI output; review material culture separately.
7. Keep source-grounded facts distinct from our creative choices and annotate substantial adaptations.

## Children of Lir — initial visual identity

All designs below are **provisional artistic interpretations**, not historically documented descriptions.

- **Lir**: adult man, shoulder-length dark brown wavy hair, short beard, strong build, deep forest cloak with bronze brooch.
- **Fionnuala**: eldest sister, long reddish-brown hair, terracotta mantle; her swan form is recognisable through composition and story narration, not invented swan marks.
- **Aodh**: eldest brother, dark curly hair, slate/forest wool clothing.
- **Fiachra**: younger boy, warm blond curls, muted earth-coloured cloak.
- **Conn**: youngest, brown hair, small stature, ochre cloak.
- **Aoife**: adult woman, dark hair, green and burgundy robe, conflicted expression; avoid simplistic visual stereotypes of evil.

## Text, audio and content rules

- Target story length 900–1,200 English words, flexible if storytelling demands.
- Preserve depth and emotional tone; do not equate disability with childishness.
- Reading and audio optional, no autoplay, text resizing and contrast controls.
- Browser speech synthesis is provisional; ElevenLabs pre-rendered audio is planned later.
- Questions at story end: 2–3 comprehension prompts, one optional reflection, and a source-aware *Behind the Legend*.
- English and Spanish share narrative events, character references and art, with carefully edited translations.
- Record first publication date only when genuinely publicly launched; Git commit date is not a substitute.

## Editorial review gates

**Research → English draft approval → Spanish adaptation → scene breakdown → artwork review → accessible reader → testing → publication.**

For each story, hold a release until source fidelity, character counting, language, readability, audio control and device layout have been checked.
